## Description

The second screening of the evening is a film from the master French film maker Jean Renoir. Based on Rumer Godden’s 1946 novel, the film is an exploration of coming of age of three young women in Bengal. Entirely shot on location, the film was Renoir’s first colour film, it uses documentary footage and staged action to create a lasting impression of relationships within a family of children and parents. A cast made mostly of children and amateurs, it was the first colour film for both Jean and Claude Renoir, the first Technicolor film shot in India, one of the first films anywhere to record its sound on magnetic tape. “Technicolor chiefs consider photography of The River the best they have ever had.” Later Eric Rohmer would write of “the most beautiful colour we have ever seen on the screen.”
