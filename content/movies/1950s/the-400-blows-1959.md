## Description

The film is in our Classics Revisited section. Tuffaut’s debut is also his most personal work. The film recreates the trials of his own childhood, portraying aloof parents, oppressive teachers and petty crime. The film marked Truffaut’s journey from a leading critic to a mjor voice of the French New Wave. A lifelong cinephile, François Truffaut first made his cinematic mark as a fiery, contentious critic for famous film journal “Cahiers du cinema” in the 1950s, denouncing the French film industry’s bloated “tradition of quality” and calling for the director to be redefined as the auteur, or individual author, of the film. Truffaut then became an auteur himself, starting with The 400 Blows, which won him the best director award at Cannes and led the French new-wave charge. His own life was tragically cut short at the age of fifty-two.
