## Description

Based on Rumer Godden’s 1939 novel, Black Narcissus tells the story of five Anglo-Catholic nuns are assigned to open a school and hospital in remote Himalayas. Under the newly appointed Sister Superior Clodagh, the group journeys to an old castle formerly used by a local Raja to house his harem. In the high, rarefied, vast and lonely spaces, the nuns strive, by hard labour, they strive to set up their mission. But the altitude and exotic atmosphere begin to take psychological toll. Black Narcissus is one of the high points of Technicolor photography; Jack Cardiff’s work on the film as well as Alfred Junge’s production design won Academy Awards, and the film is used even today as a textbook example of how colour and design enable psychological moods and sustain dramatic atmosphere. The film was the sixth production from Michael Powell and Emeric Pressburger, collectively known as The Archers, who dominated the creative side of the British film industry from 1943 through 1956.
