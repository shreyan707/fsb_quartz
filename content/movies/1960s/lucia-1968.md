## Description

"Lucía", directed by Cuban director Humberto Solás, recounted the history of a changing country through the eyes of three women. In 1895, Lucía was a tragic noblewoman who inadvertently betrayed her country for love. In 1932, she was a member of the bourgeoisie drawn into the workers’ uprising against the dictator. In the post-revolutionary 1960s, she was a rural newlywed struggling against patriarchal oppression. Shot in distinct, evocative visual styles, Solás’s sprawling triptych was a vital document of radical progress. "Lucía" was, at the time, the most expensive Cuban film ever made and endured as perhaps Cuban cinema’s crowning achievement. Humberto Solás (1941–2008) made this film in his mid-twenties as his debut, which brought international acclaim. He went on to make twenty more films during his lifetime.
